# Rationale Log

## Agin-Liebes 2021
### Article
- **IsObservational**: Cross-sectional naturalistic online survey of self-selected mescaline users (author-described as 'cross-sectional study' in Discussion limitations); no randomization or investigator-administered intervention, so coded observational (Y).
- **Condition**: Article analyzes five psychiatric/SUD subgroups (depression, anxiety, PTSD, alcohol misuse/AUD, drug misuse/DUD). Of the three condition options in scope for this review (Alcohol/Smoking/Opioid), only alcohol misuse/AUD is explicitly broken out as its own subgroup (n=72); DUD is a broad, non-opioid-specific drug-use category and smoking/nicotine is not addressed anywhere in the article, so 'Alcohol' is coded, though the study is not AUD-specific overall.
- **N**: 452 is the final analyzed sample after excluding 22 respondents who could not specify mescaline type and 3 who were under 18 (from an initial 477 who completed the survey), so this is the final analyzed N, not merely enrolled/consented N.
- **ArmScope**: Single-group naturalistic survey of mescaline users with no comparator arm or multi-arm structure; N, demographics, and the AE statement apply to the whole sample rather than one arm among several, so ArmScope is coded NA rather than psychedelic_arm/pooled.
- **AE_denominator**: The article states only that 'respondents in our study reported very low rates of adverse experiences,' citing a companion report (Uthaug et al., under review, ref 18) using the same sample for the underlying detail; no explicit AE-specific denominator is given in this article's own text (the full N=452 is plausible but not stated for the AE assessment specifically), so coded not-reported.
- **PctIndigenous**: Article discusses historical/traditional Indigenous (Native American Church) peyote use as background context but does not report what percentage of this survey's 452 respondents are Indigenous, so coded not-reported.
- **SafetyReported**: The article contains one general sentence about AE rates for this same sample (see AE01), so safety is minimally addressed even though no specifics are given.
- **AnyAEReported**: The cited sentence states adverse experiences did occur ('very low rates'), so AnyAEReported = Y rather than N or NA.
- **NumDistinctAEs**: No specific AE types or a type-level count are given in this article (detail is deferred to a companion paper), matching the 'AEs reported but not counted' convention -> 'Not numbered'.
- **WorstSeverity**: Equals the max severity across the two ae_events rows created from (1) the generic low-AE-rate statement and (2) the article's own quantitative CEQ challenging-experience data, both graded Mild.
### AE events
- **Agin-Liebes 2021_AE01**: Graded on insufficient detail: the article gives no specific AE type, count, denominator, intervention, or outcome information, only a generic characterization as 'very low rates' with low overall harm risk (referencing a companion paper, ref 18, for detail not present in this text). Per the under-specified-AE rule, defaults to Mild with SeverityIndeterminate = Y.
- **Agin-Liebes 2021_AE02**: CEQ is the study's validated measure of expected in-session challenging-experience phenomenology; total-sample mean of 0.6/5 (SD 0.7) is low. Per the severity rubric, a low CEQ score defaults to Mild by intensity alone (not graded indeterminate, since the quantitative signal is clear and no intervention/persistence/seriousness signal is present). Reported as a separate 'challenging_experience' row rather than folded into the clinical 'AE' count, per the instruction to keep CEQ-sense phenomenology distinct.

## Alper 1999
### Article
- **IsObservational**: Retrospective case series of 33 patients drawn from an existing informal, non-medical ibogaine treatment network, selected from 41 cases reviewed at a 1995 NIDA meeting; 'open label' here describes uncontrolled real-world delivery observed as-treated, not a randomized or controlled experimental research protocol, so coded observational (Y).
- **ArmScope**: Single-arm case series; all 33 patients received ibogaine with no comparator arm, so N, AE counts, and demographics are inherently scoped to the psychedelic (ibogaine) arm.
- **N**: Table 2 explicitly gives N=33 as the analyzed/reported cohort (after excluding 8 of the original 41 NIDA-meeting cases for non-opioid-dependence or missing post-treatment observation); used 33, not 41.
- **PctBIPOC**: Table 1 ethnicity reports 32 Caucasian, 1 Surinamese. Treated the single Surinamese-labeled patient as the non-Caucasian/BIPOC case (contrasted explicitly against 'Caucasian' in the same table) => 1/33 = 3.0%.
- **PctIndigenous**: Ethnicity was reported only as a Caucasian vs. Surinamese split; no indigenous-specific subgroup was reported, so coded not-reported ('.').
- **NumDistinctAEs**: Counted as distinct AE types: sweating, chills, nausea, muscle aches, mydriasis, and fatal respiratory arrest. The first five are framed by the authors mainly as persistence/incomplete resolution of opioid withdrawal signs (treatment failure) rather than novel drug-caused effects, but per the safety-dataset directive to avoid understating harm they were coded as AEs since they are adverse physical signs/symptoms documented in specific patients following ibogaine administration. Excluded the Introduction's general description of the acute ibogaine psychoactive state (panoramic recall, hallucinations, and insomnia 'responsive to sleep medication' with citation to ref. 26) because that passage describes ibogaine treatment generally via prior literature, not a finding specifically reported for this cohort of 33 patients in Results/Table 2.
- **NParticipantsWithAE**: Counted 4 distinct patients with >=1 AE: the isolated-sweating patient, the isolated-chills patient, the 27-year-old female treatment-failure case (nausea, chills, muscle aches, sweating, mydriasis), and the fatal case (nausea, muscle aches, respiratory arrest).
### AE events
- **Alper 1999_AE01**: Mild: transient, self-resolved by 48h, no intervention documented; occurrence in the treatment-failure case added no further severity signal.
- **Alper 1999_AE02**: Mild: persisted 24-48h but no intervention was documented and the patient remained opioid-abstinent; duration is short of the days-to-weeks functional-impairment threshold for Moderate.
- **Alper 1999_AE03**: Mild per rubric (transient symptom, no intervention documented). Note: this symptom occurred ~2h before the fatal respiratory arrest in one patient, but the article does not identify nausea itself as a proximate cause of death, so it was not upgraded to Severe on its own; flagged here for reviewer visibility.
- **Alper 1999_AE04**: Mild per rubric, same reasoning as AE03; co-occurred with nausea in both the treatment-failure and fatal cases but is not itself identified as causally linked to the fatal outcome.
- **Alper 1999_AE05**: Mild: an expected/commonly-assessed opioid-withdrawal sign (mydriasis) with no intervention documented; patient left the treatment setting after ~8h.
- **Alper 1999_AE06**: Severe: death is an FDA seriousness criterion (CTCAE grade 5) regardless of the postmortem causal ambiguity the authors raise (forensic exam reached no definitive cause; possible surreptitious heroin use was suggested by charred tin foil found among the patient's effects). SeveritySource set to 'rubric' because the authors treat this as the central safety concern of the series but do not apply formal CTCAE/SAE terminology. InterventionRequired coded '.' because the article does not describe what, if any, resuscitative measures were attempted between the respiratory arrest and the pronouncement of death at ~19 hours.

## Alper 2012
### Article
- **IsObservational**: Retrospective case series based on systematic review of autopsy, toxicology, and investigative reports for a consecutive series of ibogaine-associated fatalities identified 1990-2008 via a treatment-provider network, OCME referral, and literature search; no randomization or research-administered intervention, so this is naturalistic real-world data (observational), not an RCT.
- **Condition**: 15 of 19 decedents took ibogaine for opioid detoxification (the predominant indication; 4 of these were also alcohol-dependent, 3 cocaine-dependent, 1 methamphetamine-dependent); 2 used ibogaine for spiritual/psychological purposes with no substance-abuse history; 2 for unknown reasons but with a substance-abuse history. Coded as 'Opioid' based on the plurality/predominant indication, though the population is mixed and not exclusively opioid-focused.
- **Substance**: Ibogaine administered as the HCl salt in 14 cases, Tabernanthe iboga alkaloid extract in 2 (cases #4, #14), dried root bark in 2 (cases #13, #16), and a powder of ambiguous form (extract vs. root bark) in 1 (case #6).
- **N**: This is a case series comprising all 19 known fatalities temporally associated with ibogaine ingestion identified via the study's case-finding methodology; N set to 19 as every identified case is included and individually analyzed in Table 1/Results.
- **AE_denominator**: Set equal to N (19) because every identified fatality has a known death outcome (the article's core 'AE'), even though toxicology/autopsy sub-data were unavailable for some (full toxicology missing in 8, autopsy missing in 5). The true population of ibogaine users at risk (denominator for incidence) is not reported/unknown -- the authors note their broader ethnographic survey may have missed 20-30% of total treatments, but do not give a total-exposed denominator.
- **ArmScope**: Single-arm case series of ibogaine-exposed fatalities with no comparator group; psychedelic_arm applies by default since all values are restricted to ibogaine-exposed individuals.
- **PctFemale**: 4 of 19 decedents were female (15 M, 4 F) = 21.05%, rounded to 21.1.
- **PctBIPOC**: Race/ethnicity of decedents is not reported anywhere in the article.
- **PctIndigenous**: Not reported; see PctBIPOC.
- **NumDistinctAEs**: This article is a case series where death is the outcome shared by all 19 participants. Rather than counting each of the 19 individual deaths as a separate 'AE', NumDistinctAEs=7 reflects the distinct proximate-cause/event categories the authors describe across the series: (1) acute ibogaine intoxication alone, (2) multidrug intoxication (ibogaine + other abused substances), (3) cardiac event, (4) suspected pulmonary embolism, (5) witnessed seizure, (6) hemorrhagic pancreatitis, (7) duodenal ulcer hemorrhage -- consistent with the codebook's 'distinct AE type' counting convention (cf. '12 participants reported headache is 1 type'). These categories are not mutually exclusive (a single case can have both an ibogaine-intoxication proximate cause and cardiac disease as a contributing factor), so per-category participant counts sum to more than 19.
- **NParticipantsWithAE**: All 19 decedents had the qualifying AE of death (the shared/defining outcome of this case series).
- **WorstSeverity**: Severe because every AE category involves death (CTCAE grade 5-equivalent) or a life-threatening/serious event, unambiguously meeting the FDA 'death' seriousness criterion.
### AE events
- **Alper 2012_AE01**: Severe because these are deaths (meets FDA 'death' seriousness criterion). n=6 based on cases where Table 1's proximate cause of death is 'Acute ibogaine intoxication' without a co-occurring abused-drug intoxication label (cases #1, #4, #5, #6, #13, #19).
- **Alper 2012_AE02**: n=4 based on cases whose Table 1 proximate cause of death explicitly cites 'combined effects' of ibogaine and named co-ingestants: case #2 (morphine), #3 (opiates/cocaine), #16 (methadone/diazepam), #17 (fentanyl/diazepam).
- **Alper 2012_AE03**: n=6 taken directly from the article's own statement (Discussion) that 'Cardiac disease was a contributing condition or proximate cause in six deaths'; the specific six case numbers are not individually itemized by the authors beyond this aggregate count, so exact case-by-case attribution could not be independently verified.
- **Alper 2012_AE04**: n=3 taken directly from the article's statement that pulmonary thromboembolism 'was the reported cause of death in three deaths (cases #7, #12, and #18)'. The article explicitly notes autopsy was inadequate to confirm PE in all three (death-certificate/clinical diagnosis only) -- flagged here but still coded MeetsFDASerious=Y since death itself independently meets the seriousness criterion regardless of diagnostic certainty.
- **Alper 2012_AE05**: Case #10 had a witnessed generalized tonic-clonic seizure ~17h post-ingestion, discussed at length as possibly related to alcohol/benzodiazepine withdrawal (n=1). The Discussion also speculates that partial seizures 'originating from the temporal lobe' may have contributed to case #14's death, but this is explicitly hedged by the authors as one of several uncertain/possible contributing factors (alongside hemorrhagic ulcer and increased intracranial pressure) rather than a confirmed seizure, so it was not added to this AE's count -- captured instead under AE07.
- **Alper 2012_AE06**: Case #8 only; hemorrhagic pancreatitis was both the official and proximate cause of death in a patient with chronic alcoholism, occurring during ibogaine treatment.
- **Alper 2012_AE07**: Case #14 only; the autopsy was 'not conclusive regarding proximal cause of death' -- hemorrhagic complications of a duodenal ulcer, increased intracranial pressure from third-ventricle obstruction (choroid plexus papilloma), and/or temporal-lobe partial seizures were all listed as possible/contributing factors. Coded as its own AE type (duodenal ulcer hemorrhage) since that was the article's leading candidate explanation; the seizure and intracranial-pressure elements of this same case are noted here rather than double-counted as separate AE rows given how entangled and speculative they are in the source text.

## Anderson 2019
### Article
- **IsObservational**: Explicitly a 'cross-sectional, retrospective, anonymous online survey' of 278 microdosers with no experimental manipulation; Discussion states 'this study was observational, not experimental.' It is a primary study with its own participant sample (N=278), not merely a methods/codebook-development paper without a patient population — the codebook is a data-analytic product built FROM this study's own survey respondents, so all extractable fields are populated from this sample.
- **Condition**: Study population is a general community sample of psychedelic microdosers (LSD/psilocybin users recruited via Reddit), not selected on the basis of alcohol, smoking, or opioid use disorder. Reduced alcohol/tobacco use appears only as a secondary self-reported behavioral outcome for a subset, not as an inclusion criterion or study focus, so none of Alcohol/Smoking/Opioid applies; coded '.'.
- **N**: 278 is the final analyzed sample ('The sample analysed in the present study includes the 278 respondents that answered the MDBC questions'), broken into LSD-only (195), psilocybin-only (50), and both (33).
- **AE_denominator**: Challenge/AE-type reports are presented as % of 603.5 total coded challenge units, not as counts of participants who reported >=1 challenge. A subjective-importance-of-challenges analysis cites only partial subgroup counts (N1=177 LSD-only, N2=46 psilocybin-only) that don't sum to a clean total, so the exact number of respondents contributing >=1 challenge cannot be determined; coded 'Not numbered' since the concept (challenges assessed over some subset of the 278) clearly applies.
- **ArmScope**: This is a single-group observational survey with no non-psychedelic comparator arm — all 278 respondents were psychedelic microdosers. The arm-based 'psychedelic_arm' vs 'pooled' distinction (built for multi-arm trial designs) doesn't cleanly apply, so coded 'NA'.
- **PctFemale**: 31/278 = 11.2% female, from 'Most participants were male (M 237, F 31, other 10)'.
- **GenderCategory**: Preserves the reported 3-category breakdown (male/female/other) including the 'other' n=10 so it is not lost by collapsing to a binary %female.
- **PctBIPOC**: Derived as (278-234)/278 = 44/278 = 15.8% from 'white or European (N = 234, other 44)'; 'other' here is used as the non-white/European category.
- **PctIndigenous**: Not broken out anywhere in the text as a distinct subgroup of the 'other' 44 respondents; coded '.'.
- **NumDistinctAEs**: The challenges taxonomy has 11 top-level categories (44 lowest-level codes, mostly without individual stats). Of the 11 categories, 'Illegality' (29.5%) was excluded as a socio-legal/economic circumstance the authors themselves state is 'not an outcome of microdosing per se', not a health/psychological AE. 'Other perceived challenges' (10.6%) was excluded as an incoherent catch-all mixing 'no challenges reported' counter-responses, logistical concerns (remembering to dose), and misc. substance-related notes, with no single describable event. The remaining 9 categories were extracted as AE01-AE09; the 'Increased symptoms (other)' category (6.2%) was further split into AE09 (dependence/tolerance/comedown concerns) and AE10 (the explicitly author-labeled 'adverse psychological events, 0.7%') because the latter is uniquely flagged by the authors as clinically concerning and merits separate tracking. Total = 10.
- **NParticipantsWithAE**: All frequencies in the article are report-level (out of 603.5 total coded challenge reports, respondents could submit up to 3), not participant-level counts of who had >=1 challenge; no figure convertible to participants-with-AE is given.
- **WorstSeverity**: All 10 extracted events default to Mild per the severity rubric (no intervention, persistence, or FDA-seriousness signal is reported for any category), so max severity across ae_events is Mild.
### AE events
- **Anderson 2019_AE01**: Grouped physiological-discomfort sub-codes (vision disruption, temp dysregulation, numbness/tingling, insomnia, GI distress, appetite loss, headaches) as reported by the article; no per-symptom severity, duration, or intervention detail given, so graded Mild/indeterminate per the rubric's under-specified-AE default. Onset 'unknown' because this reflects an ongoing microdosing regimen (repeated low doses over time), not a single identifiable dosing session.
- **Anderson 2019_AE02**: No duration, functional-impairment persistence, or intervention detail beyond the category label 'impaired focus'; defaulted Mild/indeterminate per rubric.
- **Anderson 2019_AE03**: Coded as 'challenging_experience' (CEQ-adjacent anxiety phenomenology) per the rubric's explicit guidance that in-session-type anxiety should be flagged this way rather than folded into a generic AE count; no intervention/persistence detail given so Mild/indeterminate by rubric default (a high subjective anxiety report alone does not escalate above Mild).
- **Anderson 2019_AE04**: No detail on which pole (restlessness vs fatigue) predominated per respondent, nor duration/intervention; defaulted Mild/indeterminate.
- **Anderson 2019_AE05**: No detail on duration beyond the acute period or intervention; defaulted Mild/indeterminate despite inclusion of 'fear' descriptor, since no escalation signal (intervention, persistence, seriousness) is given.
- **Anderson 2019_AE06**: No duration/intervention detail; defaulted Mild/indeterminate.
- **Anderson 2019_AE07**: No duration/intervention detail; defaulted Mild/indeterminate.
- **Anderson 2019_AE08**: Includes dissociation/depersonalization, which could plausibly be graded higher with more detail, but the article gives no duration, functional-impairment, or intervention information, so per the rubric's explicit instruction not to default ungradeable events to Severe, graded Mild/indeterminate.
- **Anderson 2019_AE09**: Distinguishes generic dependence/tolerance/comedown concerns (this row) from the explicitly author-flagged 'adverse psychological events' (AE10); no severity-relevant detail given for this sub-component, so Mild/indeterminate.
- **Anderson 2019_AE10**: The article itself calls these 'more concerning, but rare, adverse psychological events (0.7%)' with zero further description (no named events, no intervention, no outcome). Per the rubric's explicit instruction that under-specified AEs default to Mild (to avoid overstating harm as much as under-reporting it), and that qualitative author concern language alone is not a seriousness signal, this is graded Mild but flagged SeverityIndeterminate='Y' — a human reviewer should note this is the single event in this article most likely to be underweighted by that default and may warrant the source dataset (osf.io/g5cwy/) being consulted for detail.

## Barbosa 2018
### Article
- **IsObservational**: Cross-sectional survey (Design section: '1,947 UDV volunteers... surveyed... between March 2009 and August 2011') of self-selected religious ayahuasca users, compared descriptively/statistically to an external historical normative dataset (2005 Brazilian household survey). No randomization, no researcher-administered dosing protocol, no controlled arms — participants used ayahuasca as part of their pre-existing religious practice, observed as-is. Clearly observational.
- **Condition**: The article gives essentially equal, parallel treatment to Alcohol Use Disorder and Tobacco Use Disorder throughout (title, all four results tables, both hierarchical regression sets) — genuinely a dual-focus study, not separable into a 'primary' substance by any textual signal. Coded Condition='Alcohol' as a forced single-value pick per the schema (alcohol is named first in the title and the Introduction's opening paragraphs foreground AUD before tobacco), but this is a coin-flip choice: a parallel record for Tobacco/Smoking with identical N, demographics, and design would be equally valid, and the tobacco-specific findings (Tables 3–6, tobacco regression models) are of equal weight in the article and are not captured under this Condition value.
- **N**: Article states 'A total of 1,947 members of UDV 18+ years old were evaluated' (abstract, Design), used here as the overall recruited/evaluated N. However, no single 'final analytic sample' N is given — per-variable valid-N in Table 1 ranges from 1,901 (employment) to 1,946 (geographic area) due to item non-response, and the regression models (Tables 5–6) run on still smaller listwise-complete subsets (e.g., N=1,559 for the alcohol-use-disorder model). Reported the article's own headline total (1,947) rather than any one analysis-specific N.
- **ArmScope**: This is a single-population naturalistic/religious-use survey, not a multi-arm interventional study — there is no researcher-defined 'psychedelic arm' vs. comparator arm within the study itself. The comparison group (Brazilian normative household-survey sample, n=7,939) is an external historical dataset, not a study arm sharing this article's N/demographics/AE fields. Coded 'psychedelic_arm' because N, PctFemale, and all demographic fields describe only the ayahuasca-using (UDV) population and are not pooled with the external comparison sample.
- **AE_denominator**: No AE/safety assessment of any kind was conducted or reported in this article (it is exclusively a substance-use-disorder prevalence/predictor study using SAMHSA/WHO substance-use questionnaires) — the concept of an AE-count denominator does not apply.
- **PctBIPOC**: Race/ethnicity was not among the socio-economic variables collected (per Instruments/Socio-Economic Variables: 'Age, sex, years of education, and marital and employment status were solicited') and is not reported anywhere in the results tables or text. Not reported.
- **PctIndigenous**: Not reported; see PctBIPOC — race/ethnicity/indigenous status was not an assessed variable in this study.
- **SafetyReported**: The article is entirely a cross-sectional epidemiological survey of alcohol/tobacco use and use-disorder prevalence and their association with ayahuasca ceremonial attendance and UDV membership duration. It contains no assessment, mention, or discussion of adverse events, challenging experiences, or safety outcomes of any kind — the entire Methods, Results, and Discussion sections are silent on safety. Per the missingness conventions, article silent on AE/safety entirely → SafetyReported='N', AnyAEReported/NumDistinctAEs/WorstSeverity='NA', zero ae_events rows.

## Barrett 1999
### Article
- **IsObservational**: Retrospective, self-report structured-interview study using snowball sampling to characterize naturalistic/recreational co-use of alcohol with LSD or psilocybin. No randomization, no researcher-administered drug protocol -- participants are describing past personal drug-use experiences. Classified as observational (naturalistic/retrospective self-report), not an RCT or interventional design.
- **N**: Genuine internal inconsistency in the article, not an OCR/font-encoding artifact: Methods states (in cleanly decodable text -- ligatures/punctuation only, digits unaffected) 'Of the 14 individuals contacted, three declined to participate, leaving a final sample of 11 subjects.' Results separately states, in plain spelled-out English words with no font-encoding involved, 'Nineteen males and three females were interviewed for the present study' (19+3=22). These cannot both be the same figure. I did not construct any digit-substitution/cipher theory to reconcile them (per instructions). Instead I cross-checked against a third, independently legible source: Table 1 contains exactly 17 subject rows, of which exactly 3 are coded female (subjects H, R, S) and 14 male. Results states 4 subjects were excluded entirely from interaction analysis (no intoxicating alcohol+drug co-use) plus 1 excluded for uncodable responses = 5 excluded. 22 (19M+3F) minus 5 excluded = 17, matching Table 1's row count exactly, and the female count (3) matches exactly between Results and Table 1, consistent with all 5 exclusions having been male (19-5=14, matching Table 1's male count). This three-way arithmetic convergence (spelled-out Results total, Table 1 row count, Table 1 gender counts) supports 22 as the total interviewed/enrolled sample, with 'final sample of 11' in Methods appearing to be an error inconsistent with the rest of the article. I report N=22 as the enrolled/interviewed sample; note that only 17 of these 22 had codable data for the primary LSD-alcohol comparison (fewer still for some psilocybin sub-analyses), so N should be read as enrolled rather than uniformly analyzed. Flagging for human review given the unresolved Methods discrepancy.
- **PctFemale**: 3/22 = 13.6%, using N=22 per the reasoning above (3 females stated explicitly in Results and independently corroborated by Table 1's 3 female rows).
- **GenderCategory**: See N rationale -- Results narrative (22 total: 19M/3F) and Table 1 subsample (17 total: 14M/3F) both reported to preserve the discrepancy rather than collapsing it.
- **AE_denominator**: The article never frames the single reported adverse subjective experience as part of a systematic safety assessment with a stated denominator (e.g., 'X of Y participants reported...'). All 22 interviewed participants were asked open-ended questions that could in principle have surfaced such an experience, but since no explicit assessed-denominator is stated, coded 'Not numbered' rather than assuming 22.
- **ArmScope**: Single-group naturalistic/retrospective survey with no non-exposed comparator arm -- entire sample is psychedelic-(LSD/psilocybin)-exposed by definition of the recruitment criteria (past co-use of alcohol and LSD and/or psilocybin).
- **SafetyReported**: The study was not designed to systematically assess AEs/safety (its aim was characterizing subjective drug-interaction effects via Likert ratings). However, Results incidentally reports one participant's spontaneously volunteered 'very unpleasant synergistic effect' from combining psilocybin and alcohol, which is a safety-relevant event; per the cardinal safety-dataset principle (understating harm is the worse error), this is coded as SafetyReported=Y / AnyAEReported=Y with 1 distinct AE rather than treated as absent.
- **PctBIPOC**: Race/ethnicity not reported anywhere in the article.
- **PctIndigenous**: Not reported.
- **Corpus/Prefix**: No substance/condition grouping label evident within the article text itself.
- **GeneralNote_UnrelatedNumericAnomalies**: Several other numeric strings in the article (e.g., the journal volume rendered as '04' rather than '14', and Discussion subgroup counts such as '04 LSD users' / '02 reported a complete blockade') show digit patterns that also look inconsistent with known correct values, distinct from the clean ligature/punctuation substitutions (fi->_, ff->}, fl->~, comma->\, period->[) that were straightforward to read through. Per the input-fidelity instructions, no decoding rule was constructed for these; since none of them map to a schema field (they concern LSD/psilocybin sub-analysis breakdowns not captured by this schema), they were left unresolved and do not affect any extracted value.
### AE events
- **Barrett 1999_AE01**: Description is under-specified: authors give no detail on duration, functional impact, or whether any intervention occurred (unsurprising given this is a retrospective account of a past recreational event with no clinical monitoring infrastructure) -- graded Mild by default per the rubric's under-specified-AE rule, SeverityIndeterminate=Y. Classified as challenging_experience rather than AE/both since it is subjective/in-session phenomenology from naturalistic combined use, not a clinically observed/managed event, and CEQ-sense challenging experiences are not to be silently folded into a clinical AE count. InterventionRequired=N because the naturalistic community-use setting had no clinical intervention capability/context (retrospective self-report of past personal use, not a monitored session) -- article does not describe any intervention and none was structurally possible. A separate mention by another participant of feeling 'a little bloated' after heavy drinking on LSD (representative-reports section) was not coded as a distinct AE: it is presented as a mundane, non-adverse sensation consistent with ordinary alcohol consumption rather than a notable challenging or adverse effect.

## Berlowitz 2019
### Article
- **Condition**: Study population is poly-substance dependent (cannabis 72.2%, alcohol 63.9%, cocaine/cocaine base paste 61.1%, opiates 13.9%, amphetamines 11.1%, tranquilizers 11.1% among completers) rather than an alcohol-specific SUD sample; cannabis was actually the most prevalent dependence diagnosis, not alcohol. Coded 'Alcohol' because alcohol dependence was the second-most-prevalent diagnosis and was the only substance-specific severity domain besides drug use given its own ASI composite subscale reported as a significant treatment outcome (p<.001, d=1.21); no enum option exists for 'polysubstance' or 'cannabis'.
- **Substance**: Treatment is a multi-component 'Amazonian medicine' package: purging-ritual plants, dietary-forest-retreat plants, and ceremonial plants including ayahuasca (Banisteriopsis caapi).
